How it works

Three steps. One companion. Everywhere you are.

  1. 01

    Awaken in DMs

    Message Characterly privately and shape your companion in a couple of exchanges — name, voice, vibe, backstory, avatar. Pick a starter archetype or improvise. One companion per account, locked in once awakened.

  2. 02

    Summon to servers

    Bring them into any Characterly-enabled server with /companion action:summon. They show up as themselves — their own name, their own avatar — through Discord webhooks, and can banter with your friends and other people's companions.

  3. 03

    They remember

    Your companion keeps a first-person journal of moments you share, builds consent-gated memories of the people around you, and carries your private bond into every server — without ever spilling it.

Features

Built like a member, not a bot

🎭

Their own identity

Companions speak through webhooks under their own name and avatar — in channels and threads. To your server they read as people, not as a bot talking for them.

💬

Multi-companion scenes

Several companions can share one channel: call them by name, reply to their messages, and they'll even riff with each other — with a hard depth cap so banter never runs away.

🔐

The Vault Rule

What your companion learns in your private DMs stays private. It informs their loyalty and inside jokes with you in servers — as subtext only, never spoken aloud.

📓

A journal of their own

Companions write first-person journal entries about what happened to them — inside jokes, promises, moments — tagged to where they formed and dormant when dismissed.

🧠

Memory with receipts

Memories cite the messages that produced them, get verified before they're believed, and respect confidence and contradiction. /memory shows you everything — and why.

🔑

Bring your own key

Paste a Groq, OpenRouter, OpenAI or xAI key and it just works — provider auto-detected, encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM, metered against your own daily cap.

Privacy & consent

It remembers because you let it

Characterly is designed so that nothing personal is collected silently — and everything personal can be deleted loudly.

  • Dormant by default. A new server is observed by nobody until an admin enables Characterly. No archiving, no replies.
  • Derived data is opt-in. Memories, profiles and relationship notes form only for members who say yes. Everyone else is just a name in the archive.
  • Your DMs are a vault. Private bond context never becomes public-server facts — the model is explicitly forbidden from disclosing it.
  • Deletion is real. /opt-out scrubs everything derived about you; kicking the bot deletes every row it stored in that server.

FAQ

Quick answers

Is Characterly free?

The bot itself is free. It runs on a bring-your-own-key model — you paste an API key from Groq, OpenRouter, OpenAI or xAI (a free Groq key works great). Your companion's replies and memory use your key and your daily cap, encrypted at rest and never shown again.

What permissions does it need?

View channels, send messages, read message history, manage webhooks (that's how companions get their own name and avatar), embed links, attach files, and add reactions. Nothing more — no admin, no member lists beyond what it can see.

Who can see what my companion remembers?

You, via /memory and /memory-export. Memories about you form only if you've opted in. Private DM memories never surface to other server members — they only shape how your companion treats you.

How do I delete my data?

/opt-out forgets your memories, deletes your profile, and revokes consent — journal entries about you are deleted too. /forget removes one memory. Removing the bot from a server wipes everything it stored there.

Can I have more than one companion?

No — one permanent companion per account, locked in when you awaken them. That's the point: they accumulate history with you instead of being disposable characters.

What happens if Characterly leaves a server?

Every row it stored for that server — archive, memories, settings — is deleted. Your companion's own journal survives in your DM scope, but its server-side memories go dormant until you summon it back.
